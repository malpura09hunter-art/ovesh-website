AI in Cybersecurity: Friend, Foe, or Both?

By Ovesh Malpura · Educational article

Artificial intelligence is changing cybersecurity workflows. It can help analysts process information, but it can also help attackers create convincing messages and scale reconnaissance.

AI fundamentals

Defensive uses include alert prioritisation, unusual-activity detection, malware classification, incident summarisation and code review. These systems should support—not replace—human verification.

Practical steps

Models can produce inaccurate explanations, miss novel attacks or flag harmless activity. AI-assisted phishing and automated research can also increase attacker scale.

Do not paste passwords, private keys, customer records or confidential logs into an AI service unless the organisation has approved it for that task. Define access, logging and human approval for high-impact actions.

Key takeaways

Validate generated commands and code; treat model output as a hypothesis; document data sources, permissions and human approval points.
