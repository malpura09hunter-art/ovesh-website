The Science of Strong Passwords (And Why Yours Isn't)

By Ovesh Malpura · Educational article

A strong password is difficult to guess and is not reused elsewhere. Length and uniqueness matter more than clever substitutions such as replacing letters with numbers.

Security fundamentals

Long passwords or passphrases provide more possible combinations. A unique password for every service prevents a password leaked at one site from unlocking another.

Practical steps

Avoid names, birthdays, keyboard patterns, sports teams and common phrases. These can be guessed or tested automatically.

A password manager can generate random passwords and fill them only on the intended sites. Protect the manager with a strong master credential and multifactor authentication.

Key takeaways

Consider passkeys where supported. Secure recovery email and phone routes, store backup codes safely and never share one-time codes.
