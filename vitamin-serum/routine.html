01 / VITAMIN C

The bright
antioxidant.

Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant commonly discussed in skincare for its role in protecting against oxidative stress and supporting normal collagen biology.

Chemically, Vitamin C is ascorbic acid, C₆H₈O₆. It can donate electrons to reactive species, which is one reason antioxidants are important in discussions of environmental oxidative stress. In cosmetic formulations, the form of Vitamin C, concentration, pH, packaging and exposure to light and air all influence stability.

WHAT IT DOES

Antioxidant activity, support for normal collagen synthesis and a role in maintaining a more even-looking complexion are the main scientific concepts associated with topical Vitamin C.

WHY FORMULATION MATTERS

Vitamin C can be sensitive to oxygen, light and unsuitable formulation conditions. A thoughtfully designed package and formula are therefore part of the science, not just the aesthetics.

OVESH CONCEPT

The OVESH serum uses Vitamin C as its central active for an academic exploration of antioxidant skincare rather than making a clinical treatment claim.

02 / GLUTATHIONE

The cellular
antioxidant story.

Glutathione is a naturally occurring antioxidant molecule found in cells and is made from three amino acids: glutamate, cysteine and glycine.

Its reduced form, commonly written as GSH, participates in cellular redox processes and helps maintain the balance between oxidized and reduced molecules. In skincare conversations, glutathione is often associated with antioxidant and tone-evening claims, but the evidence and effectiveness depend strongly on the delivery route and formulation.

CHEMISTRY

Glutathione has the molecular formula C₁₀H₁₇N₃O₆S. The sulfur-containing cysteine residue is important to its redox chemistry.

TOPICAL VS ORAL

Topical cosmetic products and oral supplements are different delivery systems. Evidence for cosmetic outcomes should not automatically be transferred from one route to the other.

OVESH CONCEPT

Glutathione is presented here as part of the antioxidant formulation story. The page intentionally avoids promising a specific whitening, medical or clinical outcome.

04 / ROUTINE RULES

Keep it
consistent.

PATCH TEST

When introducing a new cosmetic product, follow its instructions and consider patch testing first, especially if your skin is sensitive.

DON'T OVERLOAD

More products do not automatically mean better results. Introducing one new active at a time makes it easier to understand how your skin responds.

PROTECT THE FORMULA

Store antioxidant products as directed, close the container promptly and pay attention to changes in smell, colour or texture.

ACADEMIC NOTE

This is an educational Life Science project, not medical advice. Cosmetic ingredients can behave differently depending on formulation, concentration, skin type and individual tolerance.
